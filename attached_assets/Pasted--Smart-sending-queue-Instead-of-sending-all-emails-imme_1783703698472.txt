. Smart sending queue

Instead of sending all emails immediately, put them in a queue and let a scheduler process them.

For 250 emails over 24 hours:
	•	Total seconds in 24 hours = 86,400
	•	Interval = 86,400 / 250 ≈ 346 seconds
	•	That’s about 5 minutes 46 seconds between sends on average.

Rather than sending exactly every 346 seconds, add a small random variation (for example, ±30–90 seconds) so the pattern isn’t perfectly uniform.

2. Randomized sending order

Don’t send addresses in alphabetical order or by upload order every time. Shuffle the queue so recipients are spread out.

3. Rate limiting

Configure limits such as:
	•	Maximum emails per hour
	•	Maximum emails per day
	•	Maximum simultaneous SMTP connections (if applicable)

This makes it easy to adjust sending behavior without changing code.

4. Retry logic

Handle temporary failures differently from permanent ones.

For example:
	•	Temporary issues (mailbox busy, temporary server error): retry after increasing delays.
	•	Permanent issues (invalid address, nonexistent domain): stop retrying and remove the address from future campaigns.

5. Bounce processing

Automatically classify bounces into:
	•	Hard bounce (invalid address) → suppress future sends.
	•	Soft bounce (temporary issue) → retry later with limits.

Keeping hard-bouncing addresses on your list will quickly hurt your sender reputation.

6. Suppression list

Maintain a list of recipients who should never receive future emails, including:
	•	Unsubscribed users
	•	Hard bounces
	•	Addresses that repeatedly complain or reject mail

7. Personalized content

Personalization can improve engagement. For example:

Hi John,

instead of

Dear Customer,

Avoid misleading personalization or deceptive subject lines.

8. Tracking

Collect useful metrics such as:
	•	Sent
	•	Delivered (where available)
	•	Bounced
	•	Opened (if you use open tracking)
	•	Clicked (if applicable)
	•	Unsubscribed
	•	Complaints (if your provider supports feedback loops)

These metrics help you understand whether your campaigns are healthy.

9. Campaign management

Store campaign information such as:
	•	Campaign name
	•	Subject
	•	Email template
	•	Recipient list
	•	Scheduled start time
	•	Current progress
	•	Completion status

10. Email headers

Generate proper headers for each message, including:
	•	Unique Message-ID
	•	Date
	•	List-Unsubscribe for marketing emails
	•	Reply-To when different from From

11. Respect user preferences

Include an easy way for recipients to opt out of future marketing emails where required by law. Honor those requests promptly.

12. Logging

Keep logs of:
	•	Successful sends
	•	Failures
	•	SMTP responses
	•	Retry attempts
	•	Queue status

These logs are invaluable when troubleshooting delivery issues.

13. Warm-up controls

Make the daily sending limit configurable so you can gradually increase volume as your domain establishes a good reputation.

14. Template system

Separate email templates from your sending code. This makes it easier to edit campaigns without redeploying your application.

15. Domain authentication

You’ve already configured SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. Ensure every email your application sends is actually authenticated with those records.
